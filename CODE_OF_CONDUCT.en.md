# Code of conduct

*[한국어](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) · English*

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are acting in good faith until they show otherwise. Criticise
code, not the person who wrote it.

## What is expected

- Keep discussion technical and specific. "This breaks on a case-sensitive filesystem" is useful;
  "this is garbage" is not.
- Accept that people have different levels of experience, different native languages, and
  different amounts of time to give. A question that seems obvious to you was not obvious to
  whoever asked it.
- When you are wrong, say so and move on. Nobody is keeping score.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in the issue tracker or anywhere else the project
  is discussed.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments based on who someone is — including but not limited to
  their nationality, ethnicity, gender, sexual orientation, disability, age, religion, or
  appearance.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussion, or deliberately derailing threads.

## Scope

This applies to the issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages, and any other space where
someone is participating in this project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem through the repository's issue tracker, or privately to the maintainer if the
report itself is sensitive. Reports are read and acted on.

Responses range from a request to stop, through editing or removing the offending content, to
blocking the person from the project. Which one depends on severity and on whether it has
happened before. The maintainer decides, and explains the reasoning to the person affected.

Reporting something in good faith will never be held against you, even if the conclusion is that
no rule was broken.
